The Personalized Computer for the 21st Century

What should computers do when they know us deeply and can act on what they know?

AI systems can increasingly learn about people, adapt tools and interfaces to individual needs, and act on their behalf. This workshop brings researchers and practitioners together to decide what personalized computing should become and what it must preserve.

01

About

Personal computers have rarely been truly personal.

For decades, personalization has largely meant settings, rankings, and recommendations. Modern AI introduces a more consequential possibility: systems that learn about people across contexts, generate bespoke tools and interfaces, and take meaningful action for them.

This makes an old vision newly plausible. It also forces us to ask what these systems should know, how they should be built, and where helpful personalization becomes unacceptable inference or intervention.

03

Participation

Join the conversation.

We welcome researchers and practitioners working on personalized and adaptive interfaces, user modeling, personal agents, proactive AI, generative interfaces, end-user programming, privacy, agency, and control.

You do not need to work explicitly on “personalization.” We especially welcome perspectives that challenge its assumptions, methods, or desirability.

This is a small, discussion-oriented workshop. Applications should briefly describe your relevant experience, why you want to participate, and one question or idea you would like the group to explore.
