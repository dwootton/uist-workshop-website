The Personalized Computer for the 21st Century

What should computers do when they know us deeply and can act on what they know?

A half-day UIST 2026 workshop bringing together researchers, designers, and practitioners to imagine the next generation of personalized computing systems.

Premise

The personal computer is becoming truly personal.

For decades, computers have stored our information while understanding remarkably little about us. That is changing. Modern AI systems can infer our goals, preferences, routines, and context and use that knowledge to generate interfaces, offer assistance, or act on our behalf.

This creates a new design space, but also difficult questions. What should a computer do with a rich understanding of its user? How should that understanding be represented and controlled? When does proactive assistance become unwanted intervention?

This workshop will bring together people working across HCI, AI, personalization, user modeling, and interactive systems to develop concrete visions for what personalized computing should become.

The Questions

Three questions will organize the workshop.

  1. What should personalized computers do?

    If a system understands its user, what new interactions, tools, and forms of assistance become possible?

  2. How should they understand us?

    Should systems rely on general models learned from large amounts of behavioral data, or on deliberately designed representations of users, tasks, and contexts?

  3. How should people remain in control?

    How can personalized systems preserve privacy, agency, legibility, and the ability to inspect or correct what the computer believes?

What We’ll Do

Not just another sequence of talks.

The workshop combines short provocations, a structured debate, and an interactive session in which participants will work together to develop and critique possible futures for personalized computing.

Participants will leave with new collaborators, sharper research questions, and a shared set of ideas about where the field should go next.

Who Should Attend

Join the conversation.

We welcome researchers and practitioners interested in:

  • Personalized and adaptive interfaces
  • User modeling and general user models
  • Personal agents and proactive AI
  • Generative and malleable interfaces
  • End-user programming and customization
  • Privacy, agency, and control in personalized systems

You do not need to work explicitly on “personalization.” We particularly welcome perspectives that challenge the assumptions, methods, or desirability of personalized computing.
